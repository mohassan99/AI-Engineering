Under the Noridian (Jurisdiction D) Oxygen and Oxygen Equipment LCD (L33797), a "Group II" oxygen concentrator/home oxygen therapy qualification is one of three severity categories used to determine whether home oxygen therapy is reasonable and necessary. To meet **Group II criteria**, a beneficiary must have **both**:

1. **A qualifying blood gas study result** showing:
   - An arterial PO₂ of **56–59 mm Hg**, **or**
   - An arterial blood oxygen saturation of **89%**

   (as opposed to Group I, which requires PO₂ ≤55 mm Hg or saturation ≤88%)

2. **AND at least one of the following co-morbid conditions**:
   - **Dependent edema** suggesting congestive heart failure; or
   - **Pulmonary hypertension or cor pulmonale**, documented by pulmonary artery pressure measurement, gated blood pool scan, echocardiogram, or "P" pulmonale on EKG (P wave greater than 3 mm in standard leads II, III, or AVF); or
   - **Erythrocythemia** with a hematocrit greater than 56%.

**Testing requirements that apply to Group II qualification:**
- The "blood gas study" may be either an arterial blood gas (ABG) test or an oximetry test.
- The test must be performed by a provider qualified to bill Medicare for it (Part A provider, laboratory, Independent Diagnostic Testing Facility, or treating practitioner). Blood gas studies performed or paid for by the DME supplier are **not acceptable**.
- Testing must be performed **in-person** by a treating practitioner or other qualified medical professional (oximetry from home sleep testing/polysomnography does not qualify, except under the specific PAP-concurrent-use pathway).
- The qualifying study may be performed while the beneficiary is on oxygen, as long as the reported values still meet Group I or II thresholds.
- If both an ABG and oximetry test are done the same day under the same conditions, the ABG result governs coverage determination.

**Additional notes:**
- If Group II criteria are met, portable oxygen equipment is covered only for use while awake and during exercise (not continuously), since resting/awake saturation is above the Group I threshold.
- Coverage for a **flow rate greater than 4 LPM** for a Group II beneficiary requires an additional qualifying blood gas study performed specifically while the beneficiary is on 4 or more LPM; without this, payment is limited to the standard fee schedule allowance.
- Oxygen equipment reimbursement is bundled — all related supplies/accessories are included in the monthly rental payment, and separately billed items will be denied as unbundling.

Under Noridian's Power Mobility Devices LCD (L33789) for Jurisdiction D (California, traditional Medicare), a power wheelchair (PWC) claim must be supported by the following documentation:

**1. Face-to-Face Encounter**
- The treating practitioner (physician, PA, NP, or CNS) must perform an in-person examination focused on the beneficiary's mobility needs before the order is written.
- The encounter must be documented as a detailed narrative note in the practitioner's chart (not merely a supplier-generated checklist form), clearly identifying that a major reason for the visit was a mobility evaluation.
- Documentation should include, as relevant: history of the present condition and past medical history relevant to mobility, symptoms limiting ambulation, causative diagnoses, medications/treatments, progression of ambulatory difficulty, distance/pace the beneficiary can walk, current ambulatory aids used, what has changed to now require a PMD, ability to stand without assistance, and description of the home setting/ability to perform ADLs in the home.
- A relevant physical examination (weight/height, cardiopulmonary, musculoskeletal, strength/ROM, neurological, gait, balance/coordination) tailored to the individual's condition.
- The encounter must specifically distinguish the beneficiary's in-home mobility needs from any additional needs for use outside the home, since coverage is based solely on in-home use.
- A written report of the encounter must be available upon request.

**2. Standard Written Order (SWO)**
- Required prior to delivery of the PMD base item (Written Order Prior to Delivery, WOPD), per 42 CFR 410.38.
- The SWO may only be written after the face-to-face encounter requirements are completed.
- The same treating practitioner who performed the face-to-face encounter must be the one who writes the order for the base item (per Social Security Act §1834(a)(1)(E)(iv)).
- The SWO may also list associated options/accessories billed separately.
- If the order is not received prior to delivery, the claim is denied as not reasonable and necessary.

**3. Specialty Evaluation (for certain PWC types)**
- Required in addition to the face-to-face encounter for a Group 2 Single or Multiple Power Option PWC, any Group 3 PWC, or a power assist system.
- Must be performed by a licensed/certified medical professional (e.g., PT or OT) or a practitioner with specific training/experience in rehabilitation wheelchair evaluations.
- The evaluator must have no financial relationship with the supplier.
- Must document the medical necessity for the wheelchair and its specific features (e.g., power seating system, alternate drive control interface, power assist system), explaining why each option/accessory is needed for the beneficiary's mobility limitation.
- A written report must be available upon request.

**4. Supplier/ATP Involvement**
- The wheelchair must be provided by a supplier employing a RESNA-certified Assistive Technology Professional (ATP) who specializes in wheelchairs and has direct, in-person involvement in the wheelchair selection for the beneficiary (required for Group 2 Multiple Power Option, Group 3, and higher-group PWCs).

**5. Proof of Delivery**
- Standard DMEPOS proof-of-delivery documentation is required; services without appropriate proof of delivery are denied as not reasonable and necessary.

Failure to meet any of these documentation elements results in denial of the claim as not reasonable and necessary.

=== Query: What are the qualifying blood gas values for home oxygen therapy? ===

Under LCD L33797 (Home Oxygen Therapy), coverage is based on blood gas study results (arterial blood gas or oximetry) performed at the "time of need," falling into one of the following groups:

**Group I** (any of the following):
- Arterial PO2 ≤ 55 mm Hg, or arterial oxygen saturation ≤ 88%, taken at rest (awake) on room air; **or**
- Arterial PO2 ≤ 55 mm Hg, or oxygen saturation ≤ 88%, taken during sleep, in a beneficiary who has PO2 ≥ 56 mm Hg (or saturation ≥ 89%) while awake — in this case, oxygen is covered only during sleep; **or**
- A decrease in arterial PO2 of more than 10 mm Hg, or a decrease in oxygen saturation of more than 5%, from baseline, during sleep, associated with symptoms of hypoxemia (e.g., impaired cognition, nocturnal restlessness/insomnia).

**Group II** (requires both):
- Arterial PO2 of 56–59 mm Hg or oxygen saturation of 89%; **and**
- At least one of: dependent edema suggesting congestive heart failure; pulmonary hypertension or cor pulmonale (confirmed by pulmonary artery pressure, gated blood pool scan, echocardiogram, or "P" pulmonale on EKG with P wave >3 mm in leads II, III, or AVF); or erythrocythemia with hematocrit >56%.

**Group III**: Applies when hypoxemia does not meet Group I or II criteria, but a medical condition (e.g., cluster headaches, not all-inclusive) is supported by high-quality, peer-reviewed literature as improved by oxygen therapy.

The qualifying blood gas study must be ordered and evaluated by the treating practitioner, performed by a qualified provider (in person), and the equipment must be shown to improve the beneficiary's condition. If both ABG and oximetry are performed the same day under the same conditions, the ABG result takes precedence for determining coverage.

## Hospital Bed Coverage (LCD L33820)

Yes, a hospital bed is covered under traditional Medicare in Jurisdiction D when specific functional criteria are met, based on bed type.

**Fixed Height Hospital Bed (E0250, E0251, E0290, E0291, E0328)**
Covered if the beneficiary has **one or more** of the following:
1. A medical condition requiring positioning of the body in ways not feasible with an ordinary bed (note: elevation of the head/upper body less than 30 degrees does not usually qualify), **or**
2. A need for positioning of the body in ways not feasible with an ordinary bed in order to alleviate pain, **or**
3. A need for the head of the bed to be elevated more than 30 degrees most of the time due to congestive heart failure, chronic pulmonary disease, or problems with aspiration, **or**
4. A need for traction equipment that can only be attached to a hospital bed.

**Semi-Electric Hospital Bed (E0260, E0261, E0294, E0295, E0329)**
Covered if the beneficiary meets one of the fixed-height bed criteria above **and** requires frequent changes in body position and/or has an immediate need for a change in body position.

**Heavy-Duty Extra Wide Hospital Bed (E0301, E0303)**
Covered if the beneficiary meets a fixed-height bed criterion **and** weighs more than 350 pounds but not more than 600 pounds.

**Extra Heavy-Duty Hospital Bed (E0302, E0304)**
Covered if the beneficiary meets a hospital bed criterion **and** weighs more than 600 pounds.

**Total Electric Hospital Bed (E0265, E0266, E0296, E0297)**
**Not covered** — the height adjustment feature is considered a convenience feature and will be denied as not reasonable and necessary.

If documentation does not justify the medical need for the specific bed type billed, or if the beneficiary does not meet any coverage criteria for any hospital bed type, the claim will be denied as not reasonable and necessary.
